- (lock) Supported MVP service categories are Water, Electricity, Gas, and Waste.
- (lock) Design envelope is one district with approximately 20–30 mahallas and one monitored Telegram group per mahalla. A pilot may begin smaller, but the product must not be conceptually limited to 3–5 groups.
- (lock) The primary interface is a private web dashboard with Hokim-related, Water, Electricity, Gas, and Waste lanes. One canonical Topic may appear in multiple applicable lanes without becoming duplicate Topics.
- (lock) Opening a Topic shows an evidence drawer containing captured original Telegram messages and useful source metadata.
- (lock) Mahalla Ovozi organizes communicated resident signals; it does not verify objective truth, determine responsibility, replace human judgment, prove that an announcement is true, or record whether a problem was solved.
- (lock) MVP excludes a complaint portal, citizen application, case management, task assignment, SLA tracking, resolution workflow, formal ticketing, severity ranking, automatic action recommendations, and manual Topic editing.

- (lock) 6.1 Telegram ingestion: the MVP direction is one BotFather-created passive bot added to each approved mahalla group. The bot should receive ordinary messages using the minimum necessary access and correctly configured privacy-mode and group permissions.
- (lock) 6.1 Access boundary: bot membership does not give the hokim's personal Telegram account access to a private group. The dashboard is therefore the reliable evidence interface. Exact bot ownership, onboarding responsibility, permissions, and group-loss recovery remain unresolved.

- (lock) 6.2 Supported input: analyze ordinary human text and textual media captions. Skip bot messages, commands, empty messages, captionless media, voice content, image or video contents, OCR, document contents, and unsupported non-text noise.
- (lock) 6.2 Prefilter boundary: deterministic prefiltering may remove only structural noise. Every structurally valid human text message or caption proceeds to semantic LLM triage.

- (lock) 6.3 Semantic classification: keywords never decide admission or rejection. Classification considers meaning, intent, whether a supported current or experienced problem is communicated, and whether the content is advertising, speculation, an announcement, or unrelated.
- (lock) 6.3 Configured words may assist interpretation but cannot control relevance. For example, an advertisement offering pipe repair may mention water infrastructure but must not create a Water Topic.

- (lock) 6.4 Relevant resident signals include direct complaints, factual resident reports, rhetorical questions, frustration, sarcasm, irony, and short standalone reports when they semantically communicate a current or experienced supported issue.
- (lock) 6.4 Message length alone does not determine relevance. Short self-contained statements such as "Svet yo‘q" or "Gaz kelmayapti" may qualify.

- (lock) 6.5 Questions and speculation: a pure future-looking question such as "Bugun suv o‘chadimi?" is normally irrelevant. A question-shaped complaint such as "Svet yana o‘chirdilami? Toza jonga tegdi" is relevant when its semantic intent communicates an experienced problem.

- (lock) 6.6 Planned or informational interruption announcements alone are excluded from MVP and create no Topic.
- (lock) 6.6 A message reporting a current observation, such as electricity having been unavailable since a stated time and still not restored, may qualify. The distinction is current or experienced evidence versus planned information.
- (kill) 6.6 Removed announcement concepts: Service notice Topics, future multi-day announcement Topics, cross-day notice projection, automatic future-date display, Service notice card labels, and combined Service notice plus resident-report labels.

- (lock) 6.7 Topic definition: one underlying resident-reported situation within one district, one mahalla, and one Uzbekistan calendar day. A Topic is not a keyword cluster, broad category bucket, permanent real-world incident lifecycle, or cross-day case.
- (lock) 6.7 Two messages in the same service category may form different Topics when they describe different underlying situations.

- (lock) 6.8 Daily boundary: Topic identity includes district, mahalla, Uzbekistan calendar day, and underlying situation. Matching resets at midnight; normal Topics and direct replies never cross the day boundary; the same continuing real-world problem becomes a new daily Topic on a later day.
- (lock) 6.8 Timestamp rule: classification uses the original Telegram timestamp even when processing occurs later. Deterministic replay must preserve the original calendar-day assignment.

- (lock) 6.9 Topic creation: no configured keyword is required, but the first supporting message must be self-contained enough to identify a supported situation. A clear standalone signal may seed a Topic; fragments such as "Bizda ham", "Yana", or "Yo‘q" cannot seed one without qualifying context.

- (lock) 6.10 Follow-up precedence: a direct Telegram reply to a Topic-linked message attaches to that Topic when it remains within the same mahalla and Uzbekistan calendar day.
- (lock) 6.10 Follow-up fallback: without usable direct-reply metadata, attach a context-dependent message to the nearest preceding relevant Topic-linked message in the same mahalla and calendar day. Unrelated messages do not break the chain.
- (lock) 6.10 There is no hourly cutoff or temporary ambiguous-message pool. Occasional nearest-preceding misassignment is an accepted MVP trade-off.

- (lock) 6.11 LLM context: for each structurally valid message, provide the new message, available reply metadata, and all raw accepted Topic evidence from every same-day Topic in the same mahalla.
- (kill) 6.11 Exclude the full raw Telegram chat, previously irrelevant messages, a separate recent-message window, and old AI summaries as evidence. Do not introduce vector retrieval, semantic retrieval, compact Topic cards, or summary-only context for MVP.
- (direction) 6.11 Pilot measurement should determine whether later context optimization is needed by observing token use, latency, compute demand, long-context quality, and grouping accuracy.

- (lock) 6.12 No automatic retroactive reassessment: a vague or unassigned message is not pulled into a Topic merely because later messages provide context.
- (crack) 6.12 Controlled developer historical replay remains a separate unresolved branch and must not be confused with rejected automatic reassessment.

- (lock) 6.13 Same-day behavior: time gaps within the day do not automatically split a Topic. Later standalone signals are compared with all same-day mahalla Topics; restoration and recurrence may remain in one Topic when they concern the same underlying situation.

- (lock) 6.14 Contradictory evidence stays in the same Topic when it concerns the same underlying situation. The system does not choose which resident is correct and does not infer an automatic resolved state.
- (lock) 6.14 Summaries must preserve disagreement with cautious attribution, for example by stating that some residents reported restoration while another reported continued unavailability.

- (lock) 6.15 Topic recalculation: the canonical Topic remains the same when accepted evidence changes. Recompute derived fields from the current raw accepted evidence rather than combining an old AI summary with a new message.
- (lock) 6.15 Recomputed fields include summary, categories, anchor, latest activity, resident attribution, Hokim-related status, and other derived presentation fields. Raw captured evidence is the source of truth.

- (lock) 6.16 Dynamic categories: one canonical Topic may belong to multiple monitored categories. Categories are derived from current accepted evidence and may be gained or lost after recomputation.
- (lock) 6.16 The dashboard may show the same canonical Topic in multiple lanes, but the underlying Topic and evidence remain single and shared.

- (lock) 6.17 Topic summaries are written in Uzbek Cyrillic, remain cautious, preserve disagreement, avoid presenting resident reports as verified facts, avoid exaggerating repetition, and distinguish one resident from several residents when reasonably possible.
- (lock) 6.17 Summaries should use attributed language such as "A resident reported", "Several messages indicated", or "Residents gave differing reports". They must not assert definite service status, government failure, or resolution unless clearly framed as reported evidence.

- (lock) 6.18 Evidence count: the Topic card displays the number of retained evidence messages, such as "7 messages". It does not use deduplicated report count as the main card metric.
- (lock) 6.18 Distinct-resident count may be used internally only to produce accurate cautious summary wording.

- (lock) 6.19 Duplicate reports: near-identical repeated messages from the same sender remain separate captured evidence messages, attach to the same Topic, and count separately in the raw message count.
- (lock) 6.19 Repetition by one sender must not be described as reports by several residents. Separate Telegram delivery of the same source message must be idempotently prevented from creating duplicate evidence.

- (lock) 6.20 Topic anchor: the anchor is the latest retained self-contained meaningful message, not automatically the chronologically newest fragment.
- (lock) 6.20 A fragment such as "Bizda ham" does not replace a self-contained anchor merely because it is newer. Contradictory evidence is balanced in the summary; the anchor is not a truth statement or final status.

- (lock) 6.21 Resident identity: the evidence drawer shows @username when available, otherwise the Telegram display name. Never show or infer a resident's phone number.
- (lock) 6.21 Sender identity is captured Telegram evidence metadata, not verified civil identity.

- (lock) 6.22 Forwarded messages follow the same semantic classification flow as other text. Forwarded status is preserved as metadata but does not automatically make a message eligible or ineligible.
- (lock) 6.22 A forwarded current supported signal may qualify; a forwarded advertisement, planned announcement, or message explicitly about another location is skipped.
- (direction) 6.22 MVP accepts that a forwarded message may occasionally concern another location without making that clear rather than implementing origin verification.

- (lock) 6.23 Edited messages are ignored for MVP. The system processes and displays the text captured when the original message arrives; later Telegram edits do not trigger reclassification, Topic reassignment, summary regeneration, or anchor recalculation.
- (direction) 6.23 Accepted trade-off: captured evidence may differ from the message currently visible in Telegram after an edit.

- (lock) 6.24 Deleted messages: once valid evidence is captured, later Telegram deletion does not remove it from Mahalla Ovozi. It remains until normal Topic retention expiry.
- (lock) 6.24 No deletion synchronization or Topic recalculation is required for MVP. The dashboard must not claim that the message still exists in Telegram; a Telegram jump link may fail while the captured dashboard copy remains available.

- (lock) 6.25 Retention is Topic-level. The entire daily Topic and all its evidence expire together 90 days after the Topic's latest relevant evidence timestamp.
- (kill) 6.25 Per-message and partial evidence expiry inside a retained Topic are rejected for MVP.

- (lock) 6.26 Filter granularity is one complete calendar day. Available filters are Today, Yesterday, and a custom date range of up to seven days.
- (kill) 6.26 Last 1 hour, Last 3 hours, and Last 6 hours filters are removed. Filters show complete daily Topics rather than partial hourly slices.

- (lock) 6.27 Example: a message reporting missing water and a damaged road may create or join a Water Topic; the road complaint is ignored for classification and generated summary, the original message remains unchanged in evidence, and no Roads Topic is created.
